# Five-minute demonstration

Use the unchanged research seed so the results below are reproducible. Explain each conclusion and its limitation.

1. **Business problem — 30 seconds.** Describe screening business laptops against a team's requirements using traceable evidence. Explain that the database is a curated research sample.
2. **Overview — 30 seconds.** Show five brands, seven families and nine variants. Explain that evidence coverage is populated specification fields, not accuracy or market share.
3. **Comparison — 60 seconds.** Compare T14 Gen 6 Arrow Lake and Lunar Lake. Show why distinct platform variants matter for memory capacity and upgradeability. Explain that maximum RAM and minimum weight may not coexist in one SKU.
4. **Portfolio gaps — 60 seconds.** Set 64 GB, 1.5 kg and upgradable RAM. Show Lenovo T14 Gen 6 Arrow Lake and HP EliteBook 840 G11 as candidates. Explain why Dell is unresolved and why a known failure can exclude another product even when a separate field is unknown.
5. **Pricing — 30 seconds.** Show the dated USD 999 Apple launch observation and explain why it cannot establish a present-day price/value winner.
6. **Ask and evidence — 45 seconds.** Ask about portfolio gaps. Explain that deterministic rules provide this result. If a model is installed, ask about M4 memory and check the cited Apple summary. Explain the difference between valid citations and a guaranteed-correct answer.
7. **Power BI and validation — 45 seconds.** Show one report page, explain the CSV-refresh workflow, and describe a meaningful regression test. End with one specific limitation and the next improvement you would prioritise.

If a live source check fails because of a manufacturer's site response, explain that outcome. Do not imply that all source retrievals always succeed or fabricate a successful check.
